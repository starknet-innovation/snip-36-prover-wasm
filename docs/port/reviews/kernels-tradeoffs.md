2. **Deferred quotient integer reduction has an unencoded batch-size bound.** `build/kernels/stwo/crates/stwo/src/prover/backend/simd/quotients.rs:243` accumulates folded group products indefinitely into u64 lanes; lines 322–325 fold once then OR-pack two results into u32 lanes. For one group, four products of input <=P and canonical coefficient <P fit u64, and the folded group result is <=5P. However there is no bound or periodic canonical reduction across groups. A concrete integer counterexample uses allowed redundant zero input P, coefficient P-1, and b=0. Every four-term group contributes 4P after folding. After 2^30+1 groups (4,294,967,300 terms), accumulator 9,223,372,041,149,743,100 still fits u64 but the final fold is 6,442,450,941 (`0x17ffffffd`). Packing spills bit 32 into the adjacent lane and loses the even lane's high bit. This batch exceeds the browser's declared memory limit; it remains a correctness gap in the portable/native implementation, and its bound must be explicit. Prefer periodic canonical reduction at a small fixed interval, e.g. at most 1024 groups, with a written <=5120P accumulation bound and canonicalization before lane packing. Alternatively use a checked conservative input bound and the original field arithmetic as fallback. Test maximal/redundant-zero lane values, all remainder widths 0–3, zero terms, and synthetic high accumulator values without allocating billions of columns.

3. **New unsafe entry point has no safety contract.** `build/kernels/stwo/crates/stwo/src/prover/backend/simd/fft/ifft.rs:37` introduces public unsafe `ifft_scaled` without its own `# Safety` documentation; new loads/stores at lines 45–46 and 176–179 lack local safety arguments. `canonical_accumulator` first folds an arbitrary u64 into at most `P + (2^33 - 1)`, then into at most `P + 4`. Its final `(y + ((y+1)>>31)) & P` returns the canonical residue: below P it leaves y unchanged, and from P through P+4 it subtracts P via the Mersenne carry. All additions fit u64. Applying this normalization every 1024 complete groups and after the final remainder bounds each intermediate accumulator by `P-1 + 1024*5P`; a remainder after at most 1023 unreduced complete groups also fits this bound. Four pre-fold products remain below 2^64. Both parity values are now strictly below P before OR-packing, so no bits overlap or disappear. This closes the unbounded-accumulation counterexample independent of native batch size.
